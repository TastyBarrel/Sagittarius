How We Think

Before you solve it, understand it.

We don't start with solutions. We start with questions.

01

We don't accept the problem as presented.

A problem statement is a starting point, not a conclusion.

We don't assume the client has correctly identified the problem, that the first explanation is correct, that leadership has the complete picture, that employees have the complete picture, or that we do either.

The evidence is the authority.

02

We question everything.

Assumptions. Explanations. Incentives. Accepted narratives. Organizational structures. Behavior. Data. The original problem statement. Our own conclusions.

Nothing is exempt from the inquiry, including the inquiry itself.

03

We listen to the whole system.

Leadership. Employees. Customers. Processes. Culture. History. Data.

Listening means more than collecting opinions. We listen for what people say, what they don't say, contradictions, differences between perspectives, patterns, biases, and the gap between stated values and actual behavior.

When employees behave in a way that causes problems, we don't stop at "employees are doing X." We ask why: what conditions are producing or reinforcing that behavior. We're not optimizing for blame. We're optimizing for understanding causality.

04

We stay solution agnostic.

We don't sell a predetermined solution. We determine what we believe is the best solution for your organization, and we make sure that recommendation is supported by sound reasoning and evidence.

The recommendation is the consequence of the inquiry, not the purpose of it.

05

We trust the process.

This is the engine of the work. We form a hypothesis, test it against what we find, and revise it, as many times as the evidence demands.

Observe

Gather what's there.

Hypothesize

Form an explanation.

Test

Check it against reality.

Learn

See what holds up.

Revise

Change the thinking.

The cycle repeats until the explanation holds up against what's actually there, not until it confirms what we expected to find.

The evidence is the authority.

Not the consultant. Not the CEO. Not the loudest person in the room.
